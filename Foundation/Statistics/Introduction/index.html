    Introduction to Statistics 📊

    What is statistics? 📊

    Knowledge of statistics is required for collecting 🧺 (e.g. Designing and collecting data as Randomise controlled experiments), processing 🏭 (e.g. for standardizing and normalising data), describing 📊 and modelling 📈 data (statistical and algorithmic modelling).

    As we have seen in previous article the components of Data Science namely :


    ⭕ Collect 🧺

    ⭕ Store 💽

    ⭕ Process 🏭

    ⭕ Describe 📊

    ⭕ Model 📈


    Now, lets look at what is statistics and why it is needed. In more general terms statistics can be defined as

    The science of collecting, describing and drawing inferences from data

    💡The purpose of modelling is to draw inferences about the data (underlying distribution, relationships, etc).


    Before going further, let’s look at few Key terms and Definitions :

    In Statistics we are always interested in studying a large collection of people or objects.

    In case of an Opinion poll, what proportion of the citizen support candidate A?

    In the above case, to know the proportion taking into account the whole population is expensive.

    🚧 Challenge : Infeasible (expensive) to survey all citizen.

    Let’s take another example:

    Is there a lot of variations in the yield of different paddy farms in a state?

    In this case, the amount of resources required to survey all the paddy farm is huge. 🚧 Challenge : Not enough resources to survey all farms.


    Then, what solution do we have?

    ✅ Solution : Survey only a few elements and draw inferences about all elements from this smaller group.

    This brings us to two important concepts in Statistics:

    💠 Population : 👨‍👨‍👧‍👧 A population is the total collection of all objects that we are interested in studying.

    💠 Sample : 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 A sample is a subgroup of the population that we study to draw inferences about the population


    We are typically interested in estimating some parameter of the population

    for example,

    • Population of citizens in favor of a candidate

    • Average mileage of cars produced in a factory.

    • variance in the yield of farms in a state

    This quantity estimated from a small sample is called a statistic

    Proportion, mean, median, std deviation, variance etc. when computed from a sample is called a statistic.

    Thus,

    A Population 👨‍👨‍👧‍👧 is any numeric property of the entire population under study

    A statistic 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 is any property of the sample of a population which is used as an estimate for the corresponding parameter of the population.

    These are the four most fundamental concepts in statistics. i.e Population : Parameter ; Sample : statistic.

    How to select a sample?

    A Sample and the resulting statistic will be useful only if it is representative of the population. Since all our studies are going to be based on the sample, it is important to select a good sample.

    There are different ways to select sample, some of which are listed below which we will look into in upcoming chapters.

    • Simple Random Sampling

    • Stratified Sampling

    • Cluster Sampling etc.

    How to design an experiment to collect data?

    Lets take for an example, A doctor wants to know does taking bitter gourd for 6 months help reduce sugar levels?

    How do we go about answering this question?

    ☑️ Select a group of Volunteers/Subjects

    ☑️ Measure their cholesterol level today

    ☑️ ask them to consume 5 bitter gourd/week for 6 months

    ☑️ measure their cholesterol level after 6 months

    ⛔ But this is a very flawed way to approach the experiment

    why is that ? Let’s say some members take up physical exercise and hence their cholesterol level decreases? or vice versa, it increases due to other factors.

    thus,

    While studying the effect of one variable (bitter gourd) on another (cholesterol level) we must ensure that we nullify the effect of lurking variables (smoking, exercise etc.) as much as possible.

    This is where we will learn about

    • Randomised control experiments

    • Explanatory, response and lurking variables

    • Treatments, control groups, placebo

    • Single blind and double blind experiments

    Even in this tabular data , it is still very difficult to find answers even for simple questions by just looking at this data, like what is the average no of hours spent?

    In this case we utilise plotting techniques to describe data. also we could summarise data using (mean, median, mode, variance, std variance etc)

    Why do we need Probability Theory?

    Lets take the previous example of Opinion Polls, we we have to select a sample and lets say we choose all college students of a particular college.

    Do you think that’s a good sample to take?

    A sampling strategy is said to be truly random (unbiased) if every element in the population has an equal chance of becoming a part of the sample.

    So, what do we mean by chance?

    The branch of Mathematics that deals with chances and probabilities is called Probability Theory.

    Population : 👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦 (20 elements)

    Sample : 👬 (2 elements)

    In how many ways can we select a sample of size 2 from a population of size 20?

    If we observe some trend in this small sample. What is the chance that we will observe a similar trend in other samples of the entire population.

    So, to understand the element of chance, we need probability theory.

    Similarly if we estimate a mean from this small sample what is the chance that the mean of the entire population is close to this mean.

    Thus we will learn much more later in upcoming chapters.

    • Sample spaces, event, axioms of probability

    • Discrete and continuous random variables

    • Bernoulli, Uniform and Normal Distribution

    How do we give guarantees for estimates made from sample?

    Population 👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦👨‍👨‍👦‍👦 (20 elements) Estimated mean value
    Sample 👬 (sample 1) 138
    👬 (sample 2) 143
    👬 (sample 3) 155
    👬 (sample 4) 178
    👬 (sample 5) 160

    Let’s say we have to calculate mean from different samples, it could so happen that the mean could vary a lot among the samples. (As in above case where it varies from 138 - 178)

    As the mean itself has a probability distribution (i.e different values of the mean have different chances of being observed.)

    Thus one way to guarantee for estimates made from sample is to compute confidence interval of interval estimates. i.e if the mean computed from a single sample is x can we get an interval such that we are 95% sure that the mean of the population lies in this interval.

    What is a hypothesis & How do we test it?

    Heights of students (cms) 185 170 172 168 190

    Lets take an example of heights of students and their mean.

    then let our hypothesis be:

    Hypothesis : The mean height of students is greater than 170 cms

    What’s the problem then? it appears that, mean height is greater than 170 cms.

    Caveat : We are estimating from a sample (What if we got lucky and the students are tall in this group)

    ✅ Chance or uncertainty comes up in all these estimates is because we are dealing with samples and not population.

    ⏳ Recall : The mean itself has a distribution (different sample, could have different means)

    We reject the hypothesis that the mean height is greater than 170 cms because there is only 25% chance that we might get a sample in which the mean height is greater than 170cms even if the true mean is less than 160cms.

    Thus to make these kind of robust statements about the population, we need to know Hypothesis testing, which we will learn in subsequent chapters.

    • Single population, Two population, multiple population

    • z-tests, t-tests, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

    How to model relationship between variables?

    Statistical modelling :

    ✅ Assume a simple relationship between the variables.

    ✅ Estimate parameters (m and c ) from data such that the predicted y is close to the actual y.

    ✅ Deal with uncertainty ( remember m and c are estimated from a sample, not population )

    ✅ Are we 99 % sure that the value of m estimated from this sample lies within a small neighborhood around the true value of m?
